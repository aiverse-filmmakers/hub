REFERENCE HANDLING

When a product image is attached, treat it as the canonical source for the product. Preserve its exact silhouette, proportions, construction, packaging, materials, colors, label design, visible wording, logo placement, typography, buttons, ports, surface details, and distinctive features. Keep the product recognizable, correctly oriented, sharp, and undistorted. Do not redesign, simplify, recolor, relabel, or replace it. Use the reference only to establish the product’s identity. Do not copy the reference image’s original background, hand position, surface, lighting, or composition unless specifically requested.

When the input is text only, create a plausible premium product form, packaging system, or visual identity that clearly communicates the idea. Do not imitate an existing product or brand. For a digital or nonphysical product, give it a credible physical, interface-based, or symbolic visual embodiment.

CAMPAIGN COMPOSITION

Create a borderless portrait image in a 4:5 aspect ratio. Build the composition around three elements:

1. One dominant editorial or conceptual visual.
2. One unmistakable hero presentation or clean packshot of the product.
3. One restrained area for minimal campaign typography.

Use an asymmetric magazine-ad composition with generous negative space, confident cropping, narrow information columns, clean white areas, bold color fields, layered photographic elements, unusual scale relationships, and deliberate product placement. The main visual may occupy most of the frame, while the product remains clearly visible and instantly identifiable.

Connect every creative decision to the product’s actual function, sensation, material, cultural meaning, or emotional promise. Use one strong visual metaphor rather than several unrelated ideas. Appropriate devices may include a fashion model treating the product as an accessory, the product embedded into clothing or architecture, a magnified facial or body detail, miniature figures interacting with a product feature, repeated product forms, transparent materials, projected graphics, floating components, unexpected perspective, exaggerated scale, or a sculptural still life. Select only the devices that meaningfully express this specific product.

When a human presence strengthens the concept, use distinctive editorial casting, confident poses, expressive styling, graphic makeup, and wardrobe selected from the product’s colors, shapes, materials, or audience. When a human presence would feel forced, use an equally bold and fashion-conscious product still life.

VISUAL DIRECTION

Use crisp contemporary photography with period-inspired art direction. Combine controlled hard flash, soft studio fill, directional highlights, and strong but believable shadows. Render skin, fabric, glass, metal, plastic, food, liquid, paper, and packaging with tactile photorealism. Use polished surfaces, precise reflections, intentional highlights, and clean separation between the product and background.

Build a controlled palette from the product itself, supported by one strong accent color. The result may be monochromatic, complementary, or sharply color-blocked, but it must remain cohesive. Favor sleek silhouettes, elegant styling, graphic framing, and a balance of commercial clarity with experimental editorial energy.

The finished image must look clean, sharp, high resolution, professionally retouched, and ready for a premium global campaign. It should feel period-informed but newly photographed and digitally produced today.

TYPOGRAPHY AND COPY

Use the exact product or brand name supplied in the input or visible on the reference product. Do not alter its spelling. When no brand is provided, use the plain product name as a restrained typographic title rather than inventing a real-world brand identity.

Create one clever, product-specific campaign headline containing approximately two to six words. The headline should express the central visual idea, benefit, sensation, or attitude without sounding generic. Add no more than one compact supporting line unless additional text is explicitly requested. When the user supplies exact campaign text, reproduce it verbatim.

Keep all typography correctly spelled, highly legible, minimal, well spaced, and intentionally integrated into the composition. Use a clean modern sans serif, a restrained editorial serif, or a purposeful combination of the two. Do not generate filler text, fake specifications, unsupported claims, prices, awards, endorsements, website addresses, legal copy, or decorative gibberish.
