Before you begin

Questions worth answering first.

Where exactly do I paste the prompts?

Paste the planning, research and writing prompts into the message box inside Claude or ChatGPT. For the operating-system prompts, place the main rules inside a persistent Project or Custom GPT instruction area so they remain available across sessions. Each prompt card below tells you the correct location.

Should I use Claude or ChatGPT?

Either works. Choose the one you already understand and can use reliably. A model with web search is useful for market research. A persistent Project is useful once you need to carry approved files and client rules forward. The workflow matters more than the brand of chat tool.

Do I paste the same prompt into my video generator?

Not usually. Claude or ChatGPT first creates the strategy, hook, script and shot brief. After you approve that thinking, adapt the visual instructions for your image or video platform. If your AI chat has a trusted creative connector, it can pass the approved request directly, but you still review the result before saving it.

How do I know when to move to the next module?

Continue only when you have saved the stated outcome. Module 1 needs a niche brief. Module 2 needs an approved offer. Module 3 needs five finished ads and one portfolio link. The blue handoff panel in every module explains what the next stage receives.

Are the prices and revenue scenarios guaranteed?

No. They are modelling examples. Your price must cover your real tools, labour, revisions, usage rights, service level, taxes, sales effort and failure rate. The calculator helps you test a scenario, not predict income.

Can AI research, send and deliver everything automatically?

It can prepare repeatable work, but client claims, personalisation, payments, account access, legal risk, publishing and delivery should remain approval-gated until the process has been manually proven. Automate the stable steps, not the uncertain decisions.

Find a service market that already spends on advertising, sells high-value work and has weak digital creative you can visibly improve.

Outcome: one researched niche
What we are doing now

We are choosing one market and one decision-maker. We are not writing ads yet because the commercial target must come before the creative direction.

Why this comes first

The niche determines the buyer’s problem, customer value, legal boundaries, visual language and realistic price. Every later decision depends on it.

Where to use the prompt

Open a new chat in Claude or ChatGPT with web search enabled. Use this chat only for market research until the niche brief is approved.

What moves forward

Save a one-page niche brief containing the buyer, painful problem, customer value, live ad evidence and assumptions. Module 2 uses this file to build the offer.

Why this matters

Popular categories attract creators because they look exciting. That often means more competition, stronger existing content and less urgency. We are looking for industries where customers are valuable and weak advertising creates a visible commercial gap.

Roofing is one possible example, not a universal answer. Your job is to find a niche where one acquired customer could justify the cost of better creative.

What good looks like
Weak

“I want to make ads for restaurants because the visuals are fun.” No budget evidence, no urgent problem and no reason to choose you.

Strong

“This service has high customer value, actively buys ads and still runs old or low-quality creative.” The gap is visible and commercially useful.

Paste into: Claude or ChatGPT with web search

Use a fresh research chat. Add your country or region, industries you understand and any production limits before the prompt.

Niche research prompt
Act as a B2B positioning researcher. Identify service niches that already spend meaningful money on traditional or digital advertising but have visibly weak video creative. Use current public information. For each niche, show customer value, evidence of ad spend, common business pain, quality of current ads, seasonality, legal or compliance risk, and why one new customer could justify better creative. Rank the top five opportunities and separate verified facts from assumptions.
Where the evidence comes from

Claude or ChatGPT organises the research, but it should not be the only evidence. Check live ads, search results and real company websites yourself. Save the links beside each claim so you can separate fact from assumption.

Continue to the offer

Sell the business result, hit the buyer’s real problem, reduce the risk, add speed plus a reason to act, then state the next action.

Outcome: one testable offer
What we are doing now

We are turning the niche research into one offer a real buyer can understand and test. The offer defines what the ads will promise and what your service must deliver.

Why this comes now

Making portfolio ads before the offer is approved creates attractive work with no commercial centre. The offer gives every hook, claim and CTA the same destination.

Where to use the prompt

Continue inside the same Claude or ChatGPT project. Attach the Module 1 niche brief and add your real production costs, delivery speed and revision limits.

What moves forward

Save one approved offer with the result, problem, risk boundary, speed, price logic, bonus and CTA. Module 3 uses it to create five ad briefs.

Why this matters

Businesses do not buy “AI videos.” They buy a plausible path to more customers, booked jobs, qualified leads or stronger conversion. The creative is the vehicle, not the final desire.

The offer contains five connected parts: the desired result, the real problem, honest risk removal, believable speed plus an easy entry point and useful bonus, then one direct next action.

What good looks like
Weak

“We make five AI videos for your social media.” It describes output but gives the buyer no business reason to respond.

Strong

“We will build five ads aimed at filling your quiet weeks. You see the finished work before the agreed payment, and the first sample is ready within the test window.” Clear result, problem, risk and action.

Paste into: the same Claude or ChatGPT project

Attach your niche brief first. Add your real costs and boundaries, then replace [NICHE] before running the prompt.

Offer-building prompt
Research current pricing and offer patterns for creative agencies serving [NICHE]. Show the evidence used. Then write three short offers using this formula: sell the buyer's desired business result, hit their biggest current problem, remove as much legitimate risk as possible, add a believable delivery speed, use an easy test price or test scope, add one useful bonus, and finish with one specific call to action. Explain every discount so low pricing does not reduce trust. Mark all prices as examples until I validate my real costs and market.
Recommended tools

Use current competitor research, your actual production costs and a simple proposal document. Never build the promise around a temporary tool promotion.

Common mistakes
  • Promising customers “on autopilot” without evidence, tracking or a media-buying plan.
  • Offering “pay only when done” without defining approval, usage, payment timing and revision boundaries.
  • Using a massive discount without explaining the test or launch reason.
  • Adding a bonus that creates work but does not improve the buyer’s decision.
  • Ending with “let me know” instead of one exact next action.

Outcome: one repeatable back office
What we are doing now

We are moving the approved workflow into a persistent project that can remember the rules and produce the same safe sequence for every client.

Why this comes now

Outreach can now create replies. Before that happens, you need a controlled place for intake, verified facts, approvals, production states and delivery checks.

Where to use the prompts

Put the main operations prompt inside Claude Project Instructions, a ChatGPT Project or Custom GPT instructions. Add the lead-intake rule underneath it.

What moves forward

Why this matters

A demo can work once while failing completely at ten clients. Every reply, brief, business fact, generation request and delivery email becomes manual labour unless the system has memory and clear states.

The goal is not to automate everything. Automate the repeatable work, keep the source of client truth structured and send failed or risky items to a person.

What good looks like
Weak

A chat remembers a messy brief for one session. You copy details manually and watch every video, even when most are fine.

Strong

Each client has one verified profile. The system drafts work from that profile, checks every output, delivers approved work and routes only failures for review.

Paste into: Project or Custom GPT instructions

This is a standing operating rule, not a one-off chat request. Add your approved offer, quality bar and delivery boundaries beside it.

Operations manager prompt
You are the Operations Manager for our AI ad service. Follow this workflow:
1. Accept the client's raw brief, including spoken or copy-pasted notes.
2. Check for required facts: company name, city, services, target audience, approved offer, visual preferences, contact details, delivery email, legal restrictions and brand assets.
3. Ask for missing facts. Never invent client information.
4. Verify public business details and flag conflicts.
5. Save one permanent client profile with source notes and approval status.
6. Convert the approved profile into five ad briefs.
7. Generate only after the briefs are approved.
8. Run factual, visual, audio, brand and legal-risk checks.
9. Prepare a delivery email only for outputs that passed.
10. Move failures to Needs Review with a specific reason. Never send or publish without the required approval.
Paste into: the same project instructions

Place this below the main operations rule. It defines what the AI does when an interested reply is brought into the project.

Why this matters

Businesses need fresh creative because audiences fatigue and offers change. That can justify recurring service, but only after the first ads and delivery process produce real value.

A $2,000 monthly package with three new variations can be useful modelling input, but it is not a universal price or schedule. Your real delivery cost and client need decide the product.

What good looks like
Weak

A subscription promises endless videos with no strategy memory, cost boundary, approval process or performance learning.

Strong

A defined number of fresh angles, an angle history, approved client profile, quality gates, delivery window and clear review scope.

Quality control prompt
Run a quality-control check on every asset before delivery. Compare it with the approved client profile and brief. Verify company name, city, services, phone, email, logo, offer, spelling, aspect ratio, duration, audio, captions, call to action, required disclosures and every factual or legal claim. If it passes, mark it Approved for Delivery. If it fails, move it to Needs Review with the exact timestamp or frame, reason, severity and safest fix. Show me only failed or ambiguous assets. Never send automatically unless the client and workflow are explicitly approved for automatic delivery.

Common mistakes
  • Copying somebody else’s price without calculating real costs and service scope.
  • Generating three “new” ads that repeat the same hook and problem.
  • Automating client delivery before the checks are stable and observable.
  • Using AI QC as an excuse to stop sampling passed outputs.
  • Treating gross monthly revenue as personal income or guaranteed profit.
Action steps
  • Define the recurring productSet monthly assets, formats, revisions, rights, schedule and exclusions.
  • Build the angle historyTrack every hook, problem, proof type, format and CTA used.
  • Calculate the real priceInclude tools, labour, sales, revisions, failures, tax and margin.
  • Implement QC gatesTest every deterministic check with a known passing and failing asset.
  • Review the first month manuallyCompare automated decisions with human review before increasing autonomy.
