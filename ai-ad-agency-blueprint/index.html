Before you begin

Questions worth answering first.

Do I have to use Claude and Higgsfield?

No. Those are the tools used in the source workflow. The operating logic is portable. Use any current reasoning model, creative platform and automation layer that can perform the same job reliably. If you use Higgsfield MCP, the current official connector is listed inside the lesson.

Is the one-day unlimited trial permanent?

No. The current official MCP page advertises a one-day unlimited free trial on selected models. Eligibility, limits and pricing can change. Verify the live offer before promising a cost structure to a client.

Are $500, $2,000 a month and the revenue projections guaranteed?

No. They are source examples for shaping an entry offer and recurring model. Your pricing must reflect your market, production quality, usage rights, service level, tool costs, revisions, taxes and delivery capacity. The built-in calculator shows scenarios, not promises.

Can AI send everything without approval?

It can prepare and route repeatable work, but client-facing claims, legal risk, account access, payments and outreach should remain approval-gated until the system has real evidence and clear boundaries.

Should I contact 5,000 businesses?

No. The final expert correction is to begin with 10 to 15 businesses that have a current reason to buy. Personalise every message with one true observation and review every draft before sending.

The complete lesson

Build from the market backwards.

Do not begin by generating pretty videos. Begin with a buyer, a painful problem and a reason your first offer feels safe enough to test.

Find a service market that already spends on advertising, sells high-value work and has weak digital creative you can visibly improve.

Outcome: one researched niche
Why this matters

Popular categories attract creators because they look exciting. That often means more competition, stronger existing content and less urgency. The source workflow looked for “plain” industries where customers are valuable and weak advertising creates a visible gap.

Roofing was the example, not the universal answer. Your job is to find a niche where one new customer can justify the cost of better creative.

What good looks like
Weak

“I want to make ads for restaurants because the visuals are fun.” No budget evidence, no urgent problem and no reason to choose you.

Strong

“This service has high customer value, actively buys ads and still runs old or low-quality creative.” The gap is visible and commercially useful.

Niche research prompt
Act as a B2B positioning researcher. Identify service niches that already spend meaningful money on traditional or digital advertising but have visibly weak video creative. Use current public sources. For each niche, show customer value, evidence of ad spend, common business pain, quality of current ads, seasonality, legal or compliance risk, and why one new customer could justify better creative. Rank the top five opportunities and separate verified facts from assumptions.
Recommended tools

Use a current research-capable model, public ad libraries, search results and company websites. Claude and Higgsfield were used in the source, but the decision framework is tool-independent.

Common mistakes
  • Picking a niche because it looks cinematic instead of because buyers have budget and urgency.
  • Using one market-size number as proof of demand. Check live advertisers and real local businesses.
  • Ignoring seasonality, licensing, compliance and the value of one acquired customer.
  • Choosing a niche without enough visible businesses to research and contact.
Action steps
  • List five service nichesChoose markets where one customer is financially meaningful.
  • Inspect live advertisingSave three real ads or website examples for every candidate.
  • Score the opportunityRate spend, creative gap, urgency, customer value and compliance risk.
  • Choose one nicheWrite a two-sentence evidence-based reason for the decision.
  • Define the buyerName the decision-maker, current pain and desired business result.

Continue to the offer

Sell the business result, hit the buyer’s real problem, reduce the risk, add speed plus a reason to act, then state the next action.

Outcome: one testable offer
Why this matters

Businesses do not buy “AI videos.” They buy a plausible path to more customers, booked jobs, qualified leads or stronger conversion. The creative is the vehicle, not the final desire.

The source formula contains five parts: result, core problem, risk removal, speed plus an easy entry price and bonus, then one direct call to action.

What good looks like
Weak

“We make five AI videos for your social media.” It describes output but gives the buyer no business reason to respond.

Strong

“We will build five ads aimed at filling your quiet weeks. You see the finished work before the agreed payment, and the first sample is ready within the test window.” Clear result, problem, risk and action.

Offer-building prompt
Research current pricing and offer patterns for creative agencies serving [NICHE]. Cite the sources. Then write three short offers using this formula: sell the buyer's desired business result, hit their biggest current problem, remove as much legitimate risk as possible, add a believable delivery speed, use an easy test price or test scope, add one useful bonus, and finish with one specific call to action. Explain every discount so low pricing does not reduce trust. Mark all prices as examples until I validate my real costs and market.
Recommended tools

Use current competitor research, your actual production costs and a simple proposal document. Never build the promise around a temporary tool promotion.

Current research modelCompetitor offer auditCost spreadsheet
Common mistakes
  • Promising customers “on autopilot” without evidence, tracking or a media-buying plan.
  • Offering “pay only when done” without defining approval, usage, payment timing and revision boundaries.
  • Using a massive discount without explaining the test or launch reason.
  • Adding a bonus that creates work but does not improve the buyer’s decision.
  • Ending with “let me know” instead of one exact next action.
Action steps
  • Name the resultWrite what the buyer ultimately wants in commercial language.
  • Name the painful constraintConnect the offer to one costly or urgent current problem.
  • Define honest risk removalSet proof, approval, payment and revision boundaries in writing.
  • Price the testCalculate tool, labour, revision, tax and usage-right costs before quoting.
  • Write one next actionUse a reply keyword, booked call or short intake form, not three options.
Weekly target
This week: produce one offer you can deliver without bluffing.

Read it as the buyer. Every promise must have a method, boundary and next action.

Build the proof

Create five ads that solve different communication problems, not five cosmetic variations of the same video.

Outcome: five finished spec ads
Why this matters

A prospect asks one question before trusting a new agency: “What will the work look like?” The first five ads answer that question before you have a client.

The source set used cinematic before-and-after, humour, customer testimonial, problem-focused and premium brand styles. The point is strategic range, not a mandatory style list.

What good looks like
Weak

Five videos with the same hook, pacing and promise, changed only by music or colour.

Strong

Five distinct buyer angles, each with one problem, a relevant format, a three-second hook, accurate details and a clear next action.

Five-ad brief prompt
Create five video ad briefs for [NICHE], each using a different strategic angle: transformation or before-and-after, humour, customer story, urgent problem, and premium brand trust. Use our researched audience and approved offer. For every ad provide: the single buyer problem, the first-three-second hook, a shot-by-shot concept, voiceover or dialogue, proof required, legal or factual claims to verify, format, duration, and one clear call to action. Do not generate the final videos until I approve all five briefs.
Recommended tools

  • Confusing visual variety with strategic variety.
  • Using fake testimonials, invented customer results or unsupported savings.

  • Keeping all outputs instead of selecting only the strongest proof.

Write the outreach

Start with their company, prove you looked, show the work, then use the free sample as a filter for genuine interest.

Outcome: one personal email template
Why this matters

Busy local operators receive generic pitches constantly. Starting with your agency makes the message feel like every other sales email. Brock’s correction was simple: their company and city first, proof second, one low-friction next action last.

The free sample is not only a gift. It is a qualification step. A prospect who sends accurate business information has taken a meaningful action.

What good looks like
Weak

“Hi, we are an innovative AI agency offering affordable content solutions.” It could be sent to anyone.

Strong

The subject contains the real company and city. The first line names one true detail. The message shows proof and asks for one reply keyword to receive a sample.

Cold email prompt
Rewrite my approved offer as a short cold email for [DECISION-MAKER] at [COMPANY]. Start with their business, not mine. Use the company name and city in the subject. Open with one verified observation about their current advertising or website. Add one clean portfolio link as proof. Keep the same honest test offer. End with one CTA: reply INTERESTED to receive one free sample creative for their business. Do not invent facts, results or familiarity.
Subject: [Company Name], your ads in [City] Hey [Name], [One true observation about their current advertising, website or premium service.] I create video ads for [niche]. Here is a short portfolio showing five different angles: [one clean link] [Your approved low-risk offer in two or three sentences.] Reply INTERESTED and I will start with one free sample creative for your business.
Recommended tools

Use public company information, ad libraries, a spreadsheet or CRM and email drafts. Keep sending manual and approval-gated during the learning phase.

Company websitePublic ad libraryGmail draftsOutreach tracker
Common mistakes
  • Opening with your agency name, your tools or your personal story.
  • Using fake personalisation such as a generic compliment.
  • Attaching five raw files instead of one clean portfolio page.
  • Offering a free sample without defining what information the prospect must provide.
  • Including several calls to action in one short email.
Action steps
  • Write the subject formulaUse company name plus city or one current commercial priority.
  • Create the observation fieldRequire one true detail from a public source for every prospect.
  • Publish one portfolio linkReplace multiple raw assets with one simple page.
  • Define the sample intakeList the exact facts needed before the sample is produced.
  • Save the email as a templateKeep only the structure reusable. Personalise the evidence every time.

Build the back office

Move the workflow from a temporary chat into a persistent project with verified client profiles, intake rules, approval gates and exception-based quality control.

Outcome: one repeatable back office
Why this matters

A demo can work once while failing completely at ten clients. Every reply, brief, business fact, generation request and delivery email becomes manual labour unless the system has memory and clear states.

Nate’s key correction was not “automate everything.” It was to automate repeatable work and show a person only the failed or risky items.

What good looks like
Weak

A chat remembers a messy brief for one session. You copy details manually and watch every video, even when most are fine.

Strong

Each client has one verified profile. The system drafts work from that profile, checks every output, delivers approved work and routes only failures for review.

Operations manager prompt
You are the Operations Manager for our AI ad service. Follow this workflow:
1. Accept the client's raw brief, including spoken or copy-pasted notes.
2. Check for required facts: company name, city, services, target audience, approved offer, visual preferences, contact details, delivery email, legal restrictions and brand assets.
3. Ask for missing facts. Never invent client information.
4. Verify public business details and flag conflicts.
5. Save one permanent client profile with source notes and approval status.
6. Convert the approved profile into five ad briefs.
7. Generate only after the briefs are approved.
8. Run factual, visual, audio, brand and legal-risk checks.
9. Prepare a delivery email only for outputs that passed.
10. Move failures to Needs Review with a specific reason. Never send or publish without the required approval.
Interested-lead intake rule
When a lead replies INTERESTED, draft an intake response asking for company name, city, services, pricing range, strongest jobs, target customer, phone, email, website, brand assets and any regulated claims. When the answers arrive, verify public details, flag conflicts and save a permanent client profile. Prepare one free sample brief from the verified profile. Do not generate or send it until the brief is approved.
Recommended tools

A project or workspace with persistent instructions, an email connector, structured client records, a generation connector and deterministic quality checks. The exact stack can change.

Persistent AI projectEmail connectorClient profile storeApproval queue
Common mistakes
  • Telling AI to “figure out” missing client facts instead of asking or verifying.
  • Keeping client truth in conversation memory instead of one structured profile.
  • Allowing automatic sends before claims, names, numbers and permissions are proven.
  • Reviewing every output manually instead of building checks and reviewing exceptions.
  • Mixing prospect, sample, paying client and delivery states in one inbox.
Action steps
  • Create the persistent projectStore the service, offer, quality bar and safety rules in its instructions.
  • Define the client schemaList required facts, source, verification status and approval status.
  • Build the intake responseAsk only for information the system cannot safely research.
  • Create the review statesUse Draft, Awaiting Approval, Generating, Needs Review, Approved and Delivered.
  • Run one synthetic clientTest the full path without contacting or billing a real business.
Weekly target
This week: complete one safe synthetic client from intake to delivery draft.

Every state, validation and approval should be visible before real client data enters the system.

Build the website

Use five blocks in order: hero, proof, process, benefits and FAQ. Follow every block with the same strongest next action.

Outcome: one selling portfolio page
Why this matters

A selling website is not an art project. It is a conversation that answers the buyer’s questions in the order they appear. Within five seconds the visitor should know what you do, who it is for and what they receive.

If the entry price is unusually low, explain the real reason. Unexplained cheapness can lower trust instead of increasing response.

What good looks like
Weak

A cinematic reel, abstract agency language and a contact link hidden at the bottom. The visitor must decode the offer.

Strong

One sentence states the result, audience and deliverable. Real work plays on the page. The process, benefits and FAQs remove friction. Every block ends with one CTA.

Selling website prompt
Build a responsive website for my [NICHE] ad service using this exact five-block structure and my approved offer and portfolio:
1. Hero: answer in five seconds what we do, who it is for and what they get. Put the approved test offer directly below. If the price is unusually low, explain the genuine launch or test reason and state the payment boundary.
2. Proof: play the strongest portfolio work directly on the page and explain the service in two factual sentences.
3. Process: show the smallest set of client actions. We collect only the facts we cannot safely research, then handle audience research, scripts, production and QC.
4. Benefits: state concrete friction removed, such as no filming day, one managed workflow and clear revision scope. Do not claim guaranteed results.
5. FAQ: answer the real questions about deliverables, filming, timing, revisions, usage rights, price and contact.
After every block, repeat one strongest CTA that opens the same short intake form.
Use accessible semantic HTML, real mobile layouts, fast media, keyboard support, privacy-safe forms and no fake proof.
Recommended tools

Use any reliable website stack that supports real media, forms, analytics and safe data handling. The source used Higgsfield MCP to launch a live page and admin flow. This AI-Verse lesson page itself demonstrates the five-block structure before the modules.

Plan the launch

Find a small group of businesses that already believe in advertising and have a visible reason to improve it now.

Outcome: 10 to 15 reviewed drafts
Why this matters

The original 5,000-company blast optimises volume before learning. Jack’s correction was to begin with businesses already spending on ads, using old video or pushing a premium service with weak creative.

A small researched batch tells you whether the niche, offer, proof and message work. A mass blast mostly tells you that generic email is easy to ignore.

What good looks like
Weak

Thousands of scraped contacts, no qualification, generic messages and no record of why each company should care.

Strong

Fifteen prospects, each with a public reason to buy, one true observation, one reviewed draft and a tracked outcome.

Focused launch prompt
Find 10 to 15 [NICHE] businesses that already spend money on advertising and have a specific reason to improve their creative now, such as outdated videos, weak promotion of a premium service, inconsistent branding or active ad spend with poor proof. Use public information only. For each business, document the source, why it fits, one true detail about its current advertising, the likely decision-maker, and a personalised email draft using our approved template. Do not send anything. I will review every fact and draft. Build an outreach tracker with company, source, reason to fit, observation, contact status, reply, intake and next action.
Recommended tools

Use public ad libraries, company websites, professional directories, a tracker and email drafts. Respect platform rules, privacy law and anti-spam requirements in your market.

  • Confusing scraped volume with sales opportunity.

Design recurring delivery

Turn the successful one-off product into a recurring creative refresh, then protect delivery with deterministic checks and human review of exceptions.

Outcome: one sustainable service model
Why this matters

Businesses need fresh creative because audiences fatigue and offers change. That can justify recurring service, but only after the first ads and delivery process produce real value.

The source used a $2,000 monthly example and three new variations each Monday. Treat those as modelling inputs, not a universal price or schedule.

What good looks like
Weak

A subscription promises endless videos with no strategy memory, cost boundary, approval process or performance learning.

Strong

A defined number of fresh angles, an angle history, approved client profile, quality gates, delivery window and clear review scope.

Recurring creative prompt
Review every active client profile and the history of hooks, problems, formats and offers already used. Propose three fresh ad variations for each client without repeating prior angles. Explain why each variation is new and commercially relevant. Prepare briefs and a delivery schedule. Do not generate or email anything until the briefs and current offer are approved.
Quality control prompt
Run a quality-control check on every asset before delivery. Compare it with the approved client profile and brief. Verify company name, city, services, phone, email, logo, offer, spelling, aspect ratio, duration, audio, captions, call to action, required disclosures and every factual or legal claim. If it passes, mark it Approved for Delivery. If it fails, move it to Needs Review with the exact timestamp or frame, reason, severity and safest fix. Show me only failed or ambiguous assets. Never send automatically unless the client and workflow are explicitly approved for automatic delivery.
