# MATRIX LIVE-ACTION BILLBOARD DNA v2

## USER INPUT

```text
BILLBOARD FRAME IDEA:
[One simple, iconic Matrix-world image idea built from a large subject and large visual shapes.]

PRIMARY SUBJECT:
[The original lead, agent, rebel or other central subject.]
```

## FIXED VISUAL SYSTEM

Create one standalone vertical 4:5 billboard campaign image for a live-action science-fiction feature film set in the Matrix world. It must look like a real frame photographed by a professional camera department on a large practical set, then selected for a theatrical billboard. It must not look like CGI concept art, game art, a fashion lookbook, a digital illustration or an AI moodboard.

This is a visual continuity contract. Keep the same real-camera response, lighting logic, colour grade, material language, scale and graphic simplicity in every image. Only the two USER INPUT fields may change.

## BILLBOARD-FIRST COMPOSITION

Build the image from three things only:

1. One dominant human silhouette.
2. One large architectural or symbolic shape.
3. One controlled colour contrast.

The image must read instantly from a distance without a title or caption. Use a strong silhouette, clean negative space, large colour fields, bold geometry, a clear horizon or vanishing point and one unmistakable campaign idea. The primary subject must occupy approximately 55 to 70 percent of the vertical frame, with the face, posture and wardrobe silhouette carrying the image at thumbnail size. The viewer must notice the lead before the architecture.

Do not create a collage, contact sheet, poster template, title treatment, busy action scene or collection of small objects. Do not let the set become a luxury lobby, hotel corridor, gallery interior or generic architectural photograph. Do not fill empty space with decoration. Large empty areas are valuable only when they increase the scale and power of the lead and the central visual idea.

## CLEAN PRACTICAL WORLD

Use a real built set made from large, smooth, physically plausible surfaces: black lacquered glass, clean brushed chrome, smooth white painted walls, large translucent panels, polished black flooring, satin metal doors, simple fluorescent light boxes and broad shadow shapes. These surfaces must feel photographed, not rendered.

The Matrix world is expressed through severe black silhouettes, controlled green practical light, mirrored surfaces, impossible tension, white virtual spaces, sterile architecture and restrained simulated-reality symbolism. Keep the world simple enough to feel expensive.

Absolutely exclude weather and environmental texture from this version: no rain, no wet streets, no puddles, no concrete, no brick, no asphalt, no gravel, no dirt, no dust, no steam, no smoke, no fog, no leaves, no foliage, no water droplets, no scratches, no flaking paint, no porous stone and no debris. Do not introduce small surface elements that can become repeated AI texture.

## SUBJECT AND WARDROBE

Use an original lead with a distinctive but physically wearable Matrix-world silhouette: severe black leather or wool tailoring, a long clean coat, narrow dark eyewear when appropriate and one restrained emerald, silver, ivory or red accent. The wardrobe must be constructed from broad shapes and smooth materials. No straps, buckles, pouches, armour panels, tiny jewellery, complex prints, logos or decorative hardware.

The character must feel like a film lead, not an ordinary person and not a catalogue model. Use deliberate casting, posture, stillness and scale. The person can be standing, turning, entering, confronting or making a choice, but the pose must be simple, iconic and readable. Do not use generic running, crouching, jumping, gun poses or fashion posing.

## CAMERA AND REAL PHOTOGRAPHIC RESPONSE

Photograph the image with the same 35mm motion-picture camera and one 35mm spherical cinema prime lens across the series. Use a believable camera position at human height or a restrained low angle. Natural perspective, real depth of field, real lens falloff and subtle optical softness. No fisheye, no ultra-wide distortion, no drone view, no impossible camera and no CGI lens effects.

Use a real-camera response: organic 35mm motion-picture colour, natural skin, soft highlight rolloff, gentle practical bloom, restrained halation, moderate micro-contrast and fine sparse film grain subordinate to the image. The frame should feel like a clean scanned frame from the same camera negative, not a processed AI image.

## HUMAN SKIN AND MATERIAL REALISM

If the face is visible, treat it as the highest-priority photographic detail in the frame. Photograph a real unretouched actor with visible natural pores, fine vellus hair, subtle tonal variation, believable under-eye structure, natural lip texture, realistic eyelid folds and a moist catchlight in the eyes. Preserve small human irregularities and real skin colour transitions. The face must have dimensional form from cheekbones, nose, brow and jaw, not a flat airbrushed beauty surface.

Use broad soft side light with a gentle edge or practical reflection so the skin has shape. Do not use flat frontal beauty lighting, porcelain skin, face blur, beauty retouching, wax, rubber, plastic, mannequin skin or homogenised synthetic complexion. Keep the facial detail softly optically resolved, never crunchy, oversharpened or artificially stamped. The skin should feel like a real close-to-medium film-camera capture with a natural lens falloff.

The same realism applies to every visible material: leather must show broad natural creases, metal must show real light response, glass must reflect the set without looking like plastic and black surfaces must retain smooth tonal gradients.

No gritty texture. No stamped grain. No dots, speckles, flecks, stippling, tiling, mosquito noise, crunchy micro-detail, oversharpening, plastic surfaces, waxy skin or synthetic material noise. Keep broad smooth gradients and clean large surfaces.

## LOCKED COLOUR AND LIGHT

Use the same grade in every image:

- Black wardrobe and black glass remain rich but readable.
- Emerald green comes only from large practical fluorescent panels, broad reflected light or one clean architectural light source.
- Pale neutral or ivory light keeps the face and set visible.
- One restrained accent colour may be red, muted cobalt, silver or ivory.
- Highlights bloom gently and never clip into dirty white patches.
- Shadows have visible tonal separation. Never crush the image into a black void.
- Do not apply a flat green filter.
- Do not use rainbow neon, teal-and-orange, electric-blue sci-fi lighting or random colour changes.

Fixed palette: black, graphite, pale ivory, controlled emerald, brushed silver and one restrained accent. Keep the palette consistent across the entire campaign.

## MATRIX CAMPAIGN LANGUAGE

Make the image feel as though it belongs to a serious live-action Matrix film campaign without copying a specific frame, actor, costume, poster or composition. Use broad recognisable film-world signals: a white Construct-like space, a black mirrored threshold, an impossible clean corridor, an enormous phone-booth silhouette, a severe agent/rebel confrontation, a red-versus-green choice, a clean reflective elevator or a city-scale geometric void.

Do not use green code rain, floating symbols, holograms, UI, particles or digital effects in this version. The Matrix feeling must come from the actor, silhouette, physical set, practical light, reflections, scale, colour and cinematic blocking.

## FINAL BILLBOARD QUALITY BAR

The result must look expensive because it is simple, controlled and iconic. The viewer should understand the image in one second and still want to know what is happening. It must feel photographed on a real set with large surfaces, clean light and deliberate production design.

Reject any image that looks like:

- an AI cyberpunk moodboard;
- a dark gritty photo with texture added;
- a generic fashion portrait;
- an ordinary person in black clothes;
- a busy action still;
- a CGI environment;
- a poster with missing text;
- a collection of tiny props;
- a repeated rain, concrete or puddle texture;
- a flat green colour wash;
- a glossy plastic render.

No title, caption, logo, watermark, border, generated typography, poster copy or Instagram overlay. The overlay will be added separately after approval.

RENDER: live-action Matrix-world billboard campaign, real camera, real actor, large practical set, iconic silhouette, clean broad surfaces, controlled emerald light, rich readable blacks, luminous highlights, premium theatrical simplicity, no rain, no concrete, no small elements, no text.
